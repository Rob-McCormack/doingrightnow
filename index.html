Open the journal

Free! No sign up, login, passwords, ads or tracking. Your journal stays on your device.

Task managers organize work you haven't started. This journal records the start. Each line lowers the bar for the next one. If you drift, the last line is exactly where you left off. By the end of the day you have a record of what you actually did — not a list of things you meant to do.

Shrink the next step

Don’t write “Edit the report.” Write “Open the document — edit the introduction.” Tiny starts concept beat big plans.

Empty your working memory

Your brain is a processor, not a storage drive. Log the line so you don’t have to hold it in your head.

Anchor your attention

Your timeline is a safety net. Come back anytime and see exactly what you were just doing.

Why a single line can help

There’s research on this, not just a productivity slogan. Getting a task out of your head, shrinking it until you can start, and noticing what you just did are well-studied ways people get unstuck. This journal is simply a place to do those three things.

If you want them

Search when you need to look back, and export just those matches. A full copy is always in Settings. Notes is a scratchpad; Quick Add saves lines you reuse. Any past entry can be brought into Today — the original stays where it was.

You can also tag a line with @person, #location, or *project — a ! makes it stand out, and xx strikes it out. Example: review checkout flow *Website — search that later if you want the history.

This doesn’t need to replace your planner. Those tools can hold the plan. This one is for the part you’re actually doing.

Notes and Quick Add

Notes

The journal is one timestamped line at a time. Some things don’t belong there — a URL, a thought for after this, a reminder before bed. Notes is a scratchpad for that. It sits under Today. It auto-saves.

Notes is one overall pad, not a note for each day. Don’t expect a Notes section under Monday or Tuesday. A full export from Settings puts Notes once at the bottom, not beside each day. Search export leaves Notes out.

Don’t turn it into a second planner. You already have tools for that, and copying the same list into two places becomes the job.

You can’t change a time or reorder the day. That’s on purpose. You can edit the words of any line, and that’s useful — at the end of the day you might add @Bill or #home so search can find it later.

A few ways people use Notes:

  • the next tiny step after this one
  • a link you don’t want to lose
  • a reminder before bed
  • a name or number that popped up mid-task
  • a sentence that isn’t what you’re doing right now

You can ignore Notes. The journal still works.

Quick Add

If you type the same line often, save it once. Open Quick Add, type the line, press Enter. Tap it later to log it now, with a new timestamp.

Tags work here too, so the saved line can already include @Bill, #home, or *Website.

  • open the document *Report
  • walk #park
  • email @Bill
  • standup *Website

That’s it — a scratchpad, and a few lines you reuse. Neither is required.

Psychological Brilliance: DoingRightNow perfectly targets the specific paralysis that heavy task managers induce.
