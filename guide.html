Getting the most out of Doing Right Now

Traditional to-do lists can feel overwhelming — they’re often a backlog of everything you haven’t started yet. When you’re stuck, staring at a long list or a complex app builds anxiety before you take a first step.

Doing Right Now is deliberately simple. No backlog, no priority matrix, no future planning. The only thing that matters is the present moment — one timestamped line at a time.

Here’s how to use this journal to build momentum without the guilt.

The art of the micro-action

Anxiety thrives on ambiguity and scale. If a task feels heavy, it’s too big. Shrink it until your brain feels zero resistance — a tiny, single-line physical movement that takes less than two minutes.

Traditional to-do (overwhelming) Doing Right Now (actionable)
Clean the kitchen Put three spoons in the dishwasher
Finish the quarterly report Open the document — edit the introduction
Pay all my bills Log into the bank’s website
Reply to unread emails Read just one email from Sarah
Do laundry Pick up the blue shirt off the floor

How to build momentum

Don’t use the journal to plan your day. Use it to navigate the exact moment you’re in. You can keep your planner — this is for when the list feels too big to start.

  1. 1. Focus on the immediate present Ignore the bigger picture. Forget the final goal. Ask yourself: “What is the very first physical movement I need to make to start this?”
  2. 2. Log your single line Open the journal and type that one micro-action. One line at a time — not a list for later. Press Enter. It’s timestamped and saved.
  3. 3. Execute the movement Do the tiny thing you wrote down. If you wrote “Open the laptop,” just open it. You don’t have to commit to doing any work yet.
  4. 4. Log the next line When you move on, don’t delete the first line. Write your next micro-action on a new line and press Enter. Your feed becomes an honest trail of small starts. Need a break? Stop. There’s no half-finished checklist — just the last line showing where you were.

Action creates momentum, not planning. By giving yourself permission to focus on ridiculously small steps, you bypass the part of your brain that freezes at scale.

Come back hours later and scroll to the last line — that’s exactly where you left off. By the end of the day you have a record of what you actually did, not what you meant to do.

For tags, Notes, Quick Add, and search, open Help at the bottom of the journal (or tap ? in the Today header).

You don’t have to organize your life before you begin. Write one line. Do that.
